Claims and specialties Which out-of-network claims qualify.

Eligibility turns on three things: how the patient is covered, where and how the service was delivered, and whether the patient chose an out-of-network physician.

Claims we pursue

  • Emergency services

    Out-of-network emergency care, including post-stabilization services where the patient could not reasonably be moved. Protected under New York law for fully insured plans and under federal law for self-funded plans, regardless of network status.

  • Out-of-network physicians at in-network facilities

    Surgeons, assistants, and ancillary physicians at an in-network hospital or surgery center where the patient did not knowingly choose them. In New York these are surprise bills; under federal law they are non-emergency services at a participating facility.

  • Unconsented referrals (New York)

    Services by an out-of-network provider after an in-network physician’s referral, where the patient did not sign the required written acknowledgment.

  • Ancillary services

    Anesthesia, radiology, pathology and lab, emergency medicine, neonatology, assistant surgeons, hospitalists, and intensivists. Federal rules bar these physicians from using the notice-and-consent waiver at an in-network facility, so the dispute runs against the plan.

  • Air ambulance

    Covered by the federal process. Ground ambulance is not.

  • Underpayments outside IDR

    Older claims, self-funded plan claims outside the federal windows, and in-network contract underpayments may support ERISA appeals or contract claims instead.

Usually not eligible for IDR

  • Medicare, Medicare Advantage, and Medicaid

    No IDR. Medicare Advantage disputes go through the plan and CMS’s payment dispute process; Medicaid disputes through plan appeals and the provider contract. More on Medicare and Medicaid.

  • Patient-elected out-of-network care

    In New York, if an in-network physician was available and the patient chose an out-of-network one at an in-network hospital or surgery center, the bill is not a surprise bill.

  • Contracted services

    In New York, no fee can be awarded if the provider or an affiliate had a contract with the plan for the same service at the same location.

  • Prior authorization that flagged out-of-network status

    In New York, no fee can be awarded if a compliant prior-authorization notice identified the service as out of network.

  • No-fault and workers’ compensation

    These have their own fee schedules and arbitration forums.

Specialties we represent

  • Plastic and reconstructive surgery

    On-call and reconstructive procedures at in-network hospitals, and medically necessary procedures such as breast reconstruction and reduction.

  • Anesthesiology

    Patients choose the surgeon, not the anesthesiologist, so out-of-network anesthesia at in-network facilities is a recurring source of disputes.

  • Orthopedic, hand, and spine surgery

    Trauma call and urgent repairs at in-network hospitals often involve surgeons the patient did not choose.

  • Neurosurgery and neuromonitoring

    High-complexity procedures and intraoperative monitoring billed out of network.

  • Emergency medicine

    Large volumes of protected claims, often suited to batching in the federal process.

  • Radiology and pathology

    Ancillary and diagnostic services with high claim counts, batched where the rules allow.

  • Assistant surgeons, hospitalists, intensivists, and neonatologists

    Physicians called in without the patient choosing them, covered under both frameworks.

  • General, trauma, vascular, and cardiothoracic surgery

    Emergency and on-call surgery is a leading source of out-of-network claims.

  • Ambulatory surgery centers

    Facility claims tied to out-of-network services, where the law allows them to be disputed.
