New York IDR

Created by Financial Services Law Article 6 and administered by the Department of Financial Services (DFS) under 23 NYCRR Part 400. It covers emergency services and surprise bills under fully insured New York plans.

  • Eligible claims

    Out-of-network emergency services, and surprise bills: services by an out-of-network physician at an in-network hospital or ambulatory surgery center where the patient did not knowingly choose that physician, and certain referrals made without the patient’s written consent.

  • Not eligible

    A patient who chose an out-of-network physician when an in-network one was available; services the provider (or an affiliate) had a contract to provide at that location; services a compliant prior-authorization notice identified as out of network; Medicaid managed care disputes filed on or after May 28, 2026; and Medicare, self-funded, and out-of-state plans.

  • Filing

    The provider or plan enters the dispute in the DFS IDR portal, which assigns a reviewer (IDRE). The application and the full reviewer fee go to that IDRE. For surprise bills, the patient’s assignment of benefits is part of the filing.

  • What the reviewer weighs

    The factors in FSL § 604, including the usual and customary cost (the 80th percentile of charges for the service in the area, from FAIR Health data), any gross disparity between the fee and what the plan pays similar providers, the physician’s training and experience, the physician’s usual charge, the complexity of the case, and patient characteristics.

  • Decision

    The reviewer picks either the provider’s fee or the plan’s payment. It does not split the difference, but it may direct the parties to try to settle. The decision is generally due within 45 business days after the reviewer has everything it needs, and it binds the provider, the plan, and the patient.

  • Filing deadline

    Article 6 does not set a fixed deadline to file, but stale claims are harder to document, and separate contract claims are generally subject to New York’s six-year limit, which plan terms can shorten. We check the dates on every claim.

Federal No Surprises Act IDR

Under 42 U.S.C. § 300gg-111 and 45 C.F.R. Part 149. It covers self-funded employer plans in every state and insured plans not governed by a state process, for emergency services, non-emergency services by out-of-network providers at in-network facilities, and air ambulance.

  1. 01

    Initial payment or denial

    The plan must pay or deny within 30 days after it receives a clean claim. Remittances are to carry codes showing whether the claim is subject to the No Surprises Act.

  2. 02

    Open negotiation (required)

    Either side may start a 30-business-day open negotiation within 30 business days after the initial payment or denial. Under the 2026 rule, notices move into the federal IDR portal, and the other side must respond by the 15th business day.

  3. 03

    Initiate IDR

    If negotiation fails, either side has 4 business days after it ends to start IDR. Missing the window generally ends the claim.

  4. 04

    Reviewer selection and eligibility

    A certified IDR entity is selected and checks eligibility. Either side can object to the entity or to eligibility.

  5. 05

    Offers and fees

    Each side submits a final offer, supporting information, and fees within 10 business days after the entity is selected. A party that has not paid its fees by then is treated as not having submitted an offer.

  6. 06

    Decision and payment

    The entity picks one offer, generally within 30 business days after it is selected, with a written explanation. Any amount owed is due within 30 calendar days.

  • What the reviewer weighs

    The qualifying payment amount (QPA, the plan’s median in-network rate) and additional circumstances: the provider’s training and experience, patient acuity and complexity, market share, teaching status and case mix, good-faith network contracting efforts, and contracted rates from the prior four plan years. The QPA gets no presumption.

  • What it cannot weigh

    Billed charges, usual and customary charges, and Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP, or TRICARE rates.

  • QPA challenges

    On August 11, 2026, the Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, vacated two of the three challenged QPA-calculation provisions of the 2021 rule. Further agency guidance is expected.

  • Batching and cooling off

    Similar claims against the same plan can be batched. For disputes whose open negotiation begins on or after November 1, 2026, a batch can include up to 50 line items. After a decision, the filing party generally waits 90 calendar days before filing against the same party for the same service, then has 30 business days to file the claims that arose meanwhile.

Medicare and Medicaid

  • No IDR for either

    The No Surprises Act and New York IDR do not apply to Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, TRICARE, or VA coverage. Those programs have their own payment rules and their own balance billing protections.

  • Original Medicare

    Participating providers accept the Medicare-approved amount. A non-participating provider that does not accept assignment may charge no more than the limiting charge, which is 115% of the non-participating fee schedule amount.

  • Medicare Advantage, provider not contracted with the plan

    The provider must accept what Original Medicare would have paid as payment in full (42 C.F.R. § 422.214) and cannot bill the patient beyond plan cost-sharing. If the plan paid less than Medicare would have, the provider can file a payment dispute with the plan and then request review by CMS’s independent Payment Dispute Resolution Contractor. If the claim was denied, the provider can request reconsideration with a signed waiver of liability.

  • Medicaid

    A provider that accepts a patient’s Medicaid must accept the Medicaid payment as payment in full; the patient cannot be balance billed. Medicaid managed care disputes filed on or after May 28, 2026 are excluded from New York IDR. The routes left are plan appeals, the provider contract’s dispute terms, and complaints to the Department of Health. The measure of an underpayment is the contract or Medicaid fee schedule rate.

  • Qualified Medicare Beneficiaries

    Patients in the Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB) program cannot be billed for Medicare deductibles, coinsurance, or copayments.

Collecting an award

  • New York determinations

    A New York IDR determination is binding and admissible in court (FSL § 607). If a plan does not pay, we pursue it with DFS and in New York state court, and New York’s prompt pay law adds interest on late payment.

  • Federal determinations

    Federal awards are binding and can be vacated only on narrow grounds. But the Second Circuit (East Coast Advanced Plastic Surgery v. Cigna, Sept. 17, 2026) and the Fifth Circuit (Guardian Flight, cert. denied Jan. 2026) held that providers cannot sue under the No Surprises Act to enforce an award. Unpaid federal awards go to CMS, the Department of Labor for employer plans, or the state insurance regulator.

  • Prompt pay

    Under Insurance Law § 3224-a, a New York-regulated plan must pay undisputed claims within 30 days of electronic submission (45 days on paper), with interest on late payments at the greater of 12% a year or the corporate tax rate.
