OutOfNetworkNY is the provider reimbursement practice of Madison Law Firm PLLC. We represent New York physicians, groups, and surgery centers in New York IDR and federal No Surprises Act arbitration, and in collecting what is awarded.

When a health plan pays an out-of-network claim at a fraction of the bill, the law gives the provider a binding way to contest it. Most practices never use it.

New York’s surprise bill law and the federal No Surprises Act both protect the patient from the balance and send the payment fight to an independent reviewer. Which law applies depends on how the patient is covered, and each process has its own fees, deadlines, and evidence rules.

We handle the process end to end: sorting claims by coverage, preparing the record, negotiating with the plan, filing, and following up until the plan pays.

What we do for practices:

  • Accounts receivable review and claim triage by coverage type
  • New York IDR filings through the DFS portal
  • Federal open negotiation and IDR, including batched claims
  • Evidence packages built on the factors each reviewer must weigh
  • Follow-up on unpaid determinations
  • Underpayment claims outside IDR, including ERISA appeals and contract claims

What changed in 2026

Both systems changed this year. Updated through November 5, 2026.

  • New York: Medicaid managed care is out

    Disputes involving Medicaid managed care plans filed on or after May 28, 2026 are no longer eligible for New York IDR (Part BB of Chapter 57 of the Laws of 2026).

  • New York: State Plans are in

    For disputes filed on or after August 26, 2026, the Empire Plan and the Student Employee Health Plan are covered. The reviewer selects the amount closer to the 50th percentile of allowed amounts, and the State Plan cannot owe more than the 80th percentile.

  • New York: both sides pay up front

    The filing party pays the reviewer’s full fee with the application and the other side pays when it responds. The prevailing party is refunded within 30 days of the decision; settlements and split decisions are refunded pro rata.

  • New York: contract and prior-authorization bar

    No fee can be awarded if the plan shows it had a contract with the provider (or an affiliate) covering the same service at the same location, or that a compliant prior-authorization notice identified the service as out of network. The plan must raise this at the start.

  • New York: longer decision period

    Decisions are now generally due within 45 business days after the reviewer has everything it needs, up from 30.

  • Federal: $15 administrative fee

    For disputes initiated on or after June 11, 2026, the federal administrative fee is $15 per party per dispute, down from $115. Reviewer fees are unchanged.

  • Federal: new batching rules

    For disputes whose open negotiation begins on or after November 1, 2026, a batched dispute can include up to 50 line items, under expanded batching criteria.

  • Federal: open negotiation moves into the portal

    The 2026 Operations Rule moves open negotiation notices and responses into the federal IDR portal, with a response due by the 15th business day. These steps phase in as CMS announces each portal function.

  • Federal: courts will not enforce awards directly

    The Second Circuit held on September 17, 2026 that the No Surprises Act gives no private right to sue to enforce an IDR award, joining the Fifth Circuit. Unpaid federal awards go to the regulators.

Questions we hear most

Which process applies to my claim?

It depends on the patient’s coverage, not the insurer’s name. New York fully insured plans go to New York IDR. Self-funded employer plans go to federal IDR. Medicare, Medicare Advantage, and Medicaid are not eligible for either. The same insurer can run all of these lines, so we check each claim.

What does it cost to pursue a claim?

We handle surprise billing arbitration on a contingency basis under a written engagement letter. Each process also has filing costs: in New York the reviewer’s fee is $300 to $495 per dispute depending on the entity, and in federal IDR there is a $15 administrative fee per party plus the reviewer’s fee. In both, the losing side ends up paying the reviewer. See the full cost breakdown.

Can I bill the patient instead?

Generally no. For emergency services and surprise bills, the patient owes only in-network cost-sharing. Anesthesia, radiology, pathology, emergency medicine, neonatology, assistant surgeons, hospitalists, and intensivists cannot use the federal notice-and-consent waiver at an in-network facility.

How long does it take?

A New York decision is generally due within 45 business days after the reviewer has the full record. Federal IDR runs through fixed steps: 30 business days of open negotiation, a 4-business-day window to file, and a decision about 30 business days after the reviewer is selected. Backlogs and eligibility disputes can add time.
