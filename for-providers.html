For physicians and practices What working with us looks like.

We take over the out-of-network dispute work your billing team does not have time for, from the first claims review to the last payment.

Request a free claims reviewCall (212) 300-3191

Most underpaid out-of-network claims are never disputed because the dollars per claim look small and the process looks slow. Across a year of claims, the gap is often significant.

Our work starts with your data. We sort each claim by the patient’s coverage, because that decides whether New York IDR, federal IDR, or neither applies. Then we flag the claims worth pursuing and tell you plainly which ones are not.

We will not value a claim on billed charges. The federal reviewer may not consider them, and the New York reviewer anchors on the usual and customary cost, so a projection built on the bill misleads you. We value claims against the benchmarks the reviewers actually weigh.

How an engagement runs

  1. 01

    Free claims review

    Send a de-identified export of out-of-network claims. We estimate what is eligible and what it may be worth, and say which claims are not worth pursuing.

  2. 02

    Engagement and BAA

    We sign a written engagement letter and a HIPAA business associate agreement before any patient information changes hands.

  3. 03

    Triage by coverage

    Each claim is assigned to New York IDR, federal IDR, an ERISA appeal, a contract claim, or no action, based on plan type and funding, network status, setting, and dates.

  4. 04

    Negotiation

    Federal claims require 30 business days of open negotiation before filing. In New York we often open a dialogue with the plan before filing as well.

  5. 05

    Filing and evidence

    We file through the DFS portal or the federal IDR portal and build the submission around the factors the reviewer must consider.

  6. 06

    Payment follow-up

    We track every determination until the plan pays, and escalate unpaid awards to the regulator or court that can act on them.

What we will ask you for

  • Claim data

    EOBs or 835 remittances with allowed and paid amounts and the adjustment (CARC/RARC) codes.

  • Plan information

    The front and back of the insurance card or the plan name, and whether the plan is fully insured or self-funded.

  • Network status

    Your participation status with each plan and any contracts, including affiliates, that cover the service at that location.

  • Setting and authorization

    Where the service was performed, whether the facility was in network, whether it was an emergency, and any prior-authorization notices.

  • Patient forms

    For New York surprise bills, the assignment of benefits and surprise bill certification form; any notice-and-consent forms.

  • Your credentials and charges

    CV, board certification, fellowship training, and your usual charges for the services in dispute.

Questions we hear most

Do you need patient records to evaluate our claims?

Not at first. A de-identified claims export (CPT codes, dates, billed and paid amounts, payer, and plan type) is enough to size the opportunity. Once we are engaged and a business associate agreement is signed, we work from the EOBs or 835 remittances and the supporting records.

What does the engagement cost?

We handle surprise billing arbitration on a contingency basis under a written engagement letter that spells out the fee and how filing costs are handled. The claims review is free.

Will this hurt our relationship with the plans?

IDR is a regulated process the plans deal with every day. Filing does not affect network status or credentialing. Many disputes settle once the plan sees a complete record.
